## 01. Mission/Vision/Values (MVV)
**Purpose**: Put the organization's reason for existing, its target state and its guiding principles into words, and create a shared reference point

**Template**:
| Element | Definition | Content |
|------|------|------|
| **Mission** | Why the organization exists (reason for being) | |
| **Vision** | Where it is heading (future state) | |
| **Values** | What it holds important (guiding principles) | - |

**How to use**: Define in the order Mission (unchanging), then Vision (5-10 years), then Values (criteria for daily decisions). The Mission is the fundamental question "Why does the world need this organization?". The Vision is "What do we want to look like in the medium to long term as we work toward the Mission?". The Values define concretely "How do we choose our daily decisions and actions along the way?".

### Worked example

| Element | Definition | Content |
|------|------|------|
| **Mission** | Why the organization exists (reason for being) | Use the power of technology to build a society where everyone has equal access to financial services |
| **Vision** | Where it is heading (future state) | Become the most trusted fintech company in the Asia-Pacific region by 2030 and achieve financial inclusion for 100 million people |
| **Values** | What it holds important (guiding principles) | - User first: every decision starts from solving the user's problem - Transparency: keep information open and fulfill accountability honestly - Challenge and learning: try without fear of failure, and learn fast from it - Respect for diversity: different perspectives are the source of innovation |

### Common mistakes
1. **Confusing Mission and Vision**: Turning the Mission into a numeric target such as "100 billion yen in revenue". The Mission is not a number; it expresses the organization's reason for being (the Why). Numeric targets belong in the Vision or the business plan
2. **Values too abstract to turn into action**: Listing generic words such as "innovation" or "teamwork" does not give a basis for daily decisions. Write down "What concrete actions embody this Value?"
3. **Defined only by leadership and never adopted on the ground**: MVV requires empathy and buy-in. Involve frontline members in the drafting process, or build a mechanism of careful dialogue after drafting so people make it "their own"

### Tips
- A test for the Mission: ask "If this organization disappeared, what would society lose?"
- Limit Values to 3 to 5. With 7 or more, people cannot remember them and they become empty words
- Ranking the Values makes decisions easier when values conflict (example: when "user first" and "speed" conflict, the former wins)
- Hold a review of MVV adoption about once every six months. Sharing "a recent moment when I was conscious of the MVV" in morning meetings or 1on1s also works

### Next steps
- After defining the MVV, use **Stakeholder Analysis** (06) to design how to spread it to the people involved
- When turning Values into concrete guiding principles, link them with the strategy frameworks in the **strategy** skill to keep consistency
- If organizational culture issues come into view, analyze the structure of employee motivation with **Motivation-Hygiene Theory** (07)

## 02. Will/Can/Must
**Purpose**: Organize an individual's career and role along three dimensions: "want to do", "can do" and "expected to do"

**Template**:
```
      Will (what you want to do)
         /    \
        /  *  \
       / ideal \
Can ──────────── Must
(what you can do)  (what is expected of you)
```

| Area | Content |
|------|-----|
| **Will** (what you want to do) | |
| **Can** (what you can do) | |
| **Must** (what you should do) | |
| **Will ∩ Can ∩ Must** | <- This is the most fulfilling zone |

**How to use**: First list each area, then identify the overlaps. The area where all three overlap is "the zone where you produce results with the greatest sense of fulfillment". If the overlap is small, expand the zone by widening Can (learning), clarifying Will (self-understanding) or adjusting Must (dialogue with your manager).

### Worked example

| Area | Content |
|------|-----|
| **Will** (what you want to do) | - Be involved in launching a new business - Be involved in data-driven decision making - Gain experience leading a team - Work on something related to overseas markets |
| **Can** (what you can do) | - Data analysis and visualization in Python - Marketing operations for existing products - Business communication in English (TOEIC 850) - Project management (3 years of experience) |
| **Must** (what you should do) | - Improve KPIs for the existing business (this term's department goal) - Train and give on-the-job training to 2 junior members - Lead the new CRM tool rollout project - Prepare the monthly report and present it at the management meeting |
| **Will ∩ Can ∩ Must** | Lead the CRM rollout project using data analysis skills, and train junior members through that process. Also use the CRM data analysis foundation as a base for exploring new business |

### Common mistakes
1. **Will stays vague**: A fuzzy Will such as "I want interesting work" never reveals the intersection. Dig into "Why do you want to do that?" five times and put it into words at the level of concrete actions
2. **Underestimating Can**: People tend to overlook skills and experience they take for granted. Get feedback from the people around you, or combine with the Johari Window (04) to find blind spots
3. **Treating Must as fixed and "given"**: Must can often be changed through negotiation or proposals. Share Will and Can with your manager in a 1on1 and look for room to adjust Must

### Tips
- Have the person fill it in as preparation for a 1on1 meeting and use it as the starting point for the conversation
- Updating it every quarter lets you track career changes
- Also pay attention to the arrow from Can to Will (tried it and found it interesting) and from Must to Can (had to do it and gained the skill). This widens your career outlook
- Sharing it across the whole team helps optimize role division and job assignments among members

### Next steps
- To dig deeper into Can, combine with the **Johari Window** (04) and discover strengths as others see them
- To turn it into a concrete action plan, use the **GROW Model** (09) for a coaching-style conversation
- To analyze all team members side by side, decide the management approach with the **Will/Skill Matrix** (08)
- For career planning, combine with the goal-setting frameworks in the **strategy** skill

## 03. Need/Want Matrix
**Purpose**: Classify the demands of the organization and its members by "necessity" and "desire" to clarify priorities

**Template**:
|  | Want | Not Want |
|---|-------------|-------------------|
| **Need** | Address with top priority | Persuasion and motivation needed |
| **Not Need** | Expectations need adjusting | No action needed |

**How to use**: Need is objective, rational necessity (what the business or organization cannot do without). Want is subjective desire (what the person or team wishes for). Separating the two axes prevents over-investing in "things we want but do not need" and neglecting "things we need but do not want to do".

### Worked example (an engineering team evaluating tool adoption)

|  | Want | Not Want |
|---|-------------|-------------------|
| **Need** | Overhaul of the CI/CD pipeline (the team wants it, and it is essential for release quality) | Adoption of a security audit tool (tedious, but mandatory for compliance requirements) |
| **Not Need** | Migration to the latest framework (engineers are interested, but the current one works fine) | Adding features to a legacy internal tool (nobody wants it, and it is scheduled for retirement) |

### Common mistakes
1. **Confusing Need and Want**: Judging that "members strongly want it, therefore it is needed". The criteria for Need are business impact, risk and constraints. Evaluate separately from feelings
2. **Neglecting the Need x Not Want quadrant**: "Things that are needed but nobody wants to do" are the easiest to neglect and cause the most serious problems. Deliberately assign resources and incentives to this quadrant
3. **Stopping after sorting into four quadrants**: Classification is only the starting point. Break each quadrant down into concrete actions (who does what, by when)

### Tips
- Use it when consolidating team requests, so you can explain the allocation of limited resources objectively
- For Need x Not Want items, carefully tell the story of "why it is needed". The thinking behind Motivation-Hygiene Theory (07) helps here
- Use it in quarterly initiative reviews to reconsider "initiatives that are fun to do but have little effect"
- With multiple stakeholders, lay each one's Need/Want side by side to find an opening for consensus

### Next steps
- If motivating members is the problem for Need x Not Want items, use **Motivation-Hygiene Theory** (07)
- If Need/Want conflicts between multiple stakeholders, design the response with **Stakeholder Analysis** (06)
- If unsure how to prioritize, use the problem-solving frameworks in the **problem** skill (Logic Tree or priority matrix)
- For the execution plan of an initiative, use the project management frameworks in the **execution** skill

## 04. Johari Window
**Purpose**: Visualize the gap between self-perception and how others perceive you, and deepen mutual understanding

**Template**:
|  | Known to self | Not known to self |
|---|---------------|-------------|
| **Known to others** | Open window (Open) | Blind window (Blind) |
| **Not known to others** | Hidden window (Hidden) | Unknown window (Unknown) |

**How to use**: Widening the "open window" is the key to building trust. Widen it through feedback (blind window to open) and self-disclosure (hidden window to open).

### Worked example (sales manager A)

|  | Known to self | Not known to self |
|---|---------------|-------------|
| **Known to others** | **Open window**: strong proposal skills, strong focus on the numbers, good presenter, early riser who comes in early | **Blind window**: tends to interrupt others in meetings, gives off an atmosphere that makes it hard for junior members to ask questions, many errors in the details of documents |
| **Not known to others** | **Hidden window**: actually lacks confidence in management, wants to go independent in the future, cannot sleep in months when the numbers are missed | **Unknown window**: has a talent for writing (never tried yet), well suited to mentoring (no opportunity yet) |

### Common mistakes
1. **Running it before people are ready to receive feedback**: In a team with low psychological safety, suddenly saying "let's point out each other's blind spots" triggers defensive reactions and backfires. Start with self-disclosure from the "hidden window" and build trust first
2. **Focusing only on negative information**: People tend to see the blind window as criticism. Center the feedback on "strengths you have not noticed yet" (positive blind spots) and it becomes easier to accept
3. **Doing it only once**: The Johari Window is something to update continuously. Running it regularly (for example once a quarter) and tracking changes gives a sense of growth and deepens trust

### Tips
- When running it as a whole-team workshop, have each person first write down 10 of their own traits, then 10 traits of each other person, and match them up to fill the four quadrants
- In a 1on1, the manager gives feedback on "what I have noticed about you recently" and the member self-discloses "what I have actually been thinking"
- To explore the "unknown window", deliberately create opportunities to take on work or roles that differ from the usual
- Deliver blind-window feedback in the "SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact)" format so it is concrete and easy to receive

### Next steps
- Update **Will/Can/Must** (02) based on the strengths and issues discovered in the blind window
- To refine how you deliver feedback, use the frameworks in the **communication** skill
- Once mutual understanding across the team has deepened, diagnose leadership styles with **PM Theory** (05)
- Design the action plan for opening up the unknown window with the **GROW Model** (09)

## 05. PM Theory
**Purpose**: Diagnose leadership along two axes: "goal achievement function (P: Performance)" and "group maintenance function (M: Maintenance)"

**Template**:
|  | M function: high | M function: low |
|---|---------|---------|
| **P function: high** | **PM type** (ideal) | **Pm type** (results-focused) |
| **P function: low** | **pM type** (relationship-focused) | **pm type** (needs improvement) |

**Self-diagnosis**:
- P function (goal setting, planning, instruction, evaluation): high/medium/low
- M function (listening, consideration, conflict resolution, team building): high/medium/low
- Current type: 

**Detailed checklist**:

| P function element | Self-rating (1-5) | Example behavior |
|------------|---------------|-------------|
| Clarifying goals | | Defines team goals in numbers and shares them with everyone |
| Planning and sequencing | | Sets task priorities and deadlines and tracks progress |
| Precise instructions | | Clearly communicates the expected deliverables and quality standards to members |
| Evaluation and feedback | | Evaluates results quantitatively and communicates improvement points concretely |
| Driving problem solving | | Makes quick judgments when obstacles arise and executes countermeasures |

| M function element | Self-rating (1-5) | Example behavior |
|------------|---------------|-------------|
| Listening and empathy | | Listens to members all the way through and acknowledges their feelings |
| Individual consideration | | Understands each member's circumstances and situation and adapts the response |
| Conflict resolution | | Detects conflicts between members early and mediates and adjusts |
| Building team cohesion | | Celebrates team successes with everyone and strengthens the sense of belonging |
| Securing psychological safety | | Does not blame failure and creates an atmosphere that encourages challenge and speaking up |

### Worked example (manager B of a product development team)

- P function: high (clear goal setting, high accuracy in sprint planning, weekly KPI reviews)
- M function: low (1on1s have become a formality, slow to notice member dissatisfaction, tends to leave conflicts within the team unaddressed)
- Current type: **Pm type** (results-focused)
- Issue: members keep leaving. Results are coming in, but the team atmosphere is deteriorating
- Improvement direction: strengthen the M function. Specifically, raise the quality of 1on1s (focus on listening), hold a team-building event once a month, and check on changes in members' emotional state weekly

### Common mistakes
1. **Treating the PM type as universal and always aiming for it**: Some situations call for Pm-type leadership (crisis response, short-term turnaround, etc.). What matters is identifying "what the team lacks right now"
2. **Seeing the P and M functions as a trade-off**: The dichotomy "being strict makes people dislike you" and "being kind means no results" is a misunderstanding. The P and M functions are independent axes, and both can be raised at the same time
3. **Diagnosing by self-assessment alone**: There is a gap between how the leader sees themselves and how members see them. Combine with 360-degree feedback or the Johari Window (04) for the diagnosis

### Tips
- As stages of leader growth, the practical order is to acquire the P function first (the ability to get work done), then raise the M function (the ability to bring out the best in people)
- For a Pm-type leader to raise the M function, commit thoroughly to just one thing: "listening". Set a rule to keep your own talking time in 1on1s under 30% of the total
- For a pM-type leader to raise the P function, commit thoroughly to just one thing: "no vague instructions". Always state "what, by when, at what quality" when making a request
- To raise leadership capability across the whole team, make PM Theory a shared language and have members exchange P/M function feedback with each other

### Next steps
- If strengthening the M function is the issue, refine coaching skills with the **GROW Model** (09)
- Design management methods for individual members with the **Will/Skill Matrix** (08)
- To deepen self-understanding as a leader, receive 360-degree feedback with the **Johari Window** (04)
- To structure team-wide issues, use the problem-solving frameworks in the **problem** skill

## 06. Stakeholder Analysis
**Purpose**: Identify the stakeholders involved in a project and decide how to engage each one according to their influence and level of interest

**Template**:
| Stakeholder | Interests | Influence | Interest level | Engagement approach |
|--------------|-------|-------|-------|---------|
| | | high/low | high/low | |

**Engagement matrix**:
|  | Interest level: high | Interest level: low |
|---|----------|----------|
| **Influence: high** | Manage closely (Key Player) | Keep satisfied |
| **Influence: low** | Keep informed | Monitor |

**How to use**: (1) Brainstorm to list stakeholders exhaustively (2) Organize each one's interests (3) Map them onto four quadrants by influence and interest level (4) Decide the engagement approach and communication frequency for each quadrant.

### Worked example (internal DX promotion project)

| Stakeholder | Interests | Influence | Interest level | Engagement approach |
|--------------|-------|-------|-------|---------|
| CEO | ROI, competitive advantage | High | High | Monthly progress reports, demos at the management meeting. Obtain approval for decisions |
| CFO | Return on investment, cost control | High | Medium | Submit a cost-benefit report every quarter. Share budget overrun risks early |
| Head of information systems | Security, consistency with existing systems | High | High | Weekly technical consultations. Always have them attend design reviews |
| Sales division (user division) | Changes in workload, usability | Low | High | Hold monthly briefings. Select pilot users and collect feedback early |
| External vendor | Contract scope, schedule | Medium | High | Confirm progress in weekly regular meetings. Agree scope changes in writing |
| HR department | Staffing, education and training | Low | Low | Share information as needed. Consult only when there is a major organizational change |

### Common mistakes
1. **Overlooking stakeholders who are hard to see**: List not only direct stakeholders but also people affected indirectly (adjacent departments, customers, unions, external regulators, etc.). Asking "Who is inconvenienced by this change?" reduces omissions
2. **Treating influence and interest level as static**: When the project phase changes, each stakeholder's influence and interest level shift too. Review at each phase (planning, design, execution, adoption)
3. **Focusing on Key Players and neglecting the other quadrants**: Underestimating the "low influence but high interest" group (frontline users, etc.) leads to resistance after rollout and the project becoming a formality

### Tips
- Do the initial listing "while looking at the organization chart" to reduce omissions. Do not forget people outside the organization (customers, business partners, regulators)
- Also record each stakeholder's stance (for/neutral/against) to make it easier to plan how to build allies
- For stakeholders who are against, interview each one about "what would need to change for you to be in favor"
- Decide the frequency, channel and owner of communication in the engagement approach so that execution does not stall

### Next steps
- For negotiating with and persuading Key Players, use the frameworks in the **communication** skill (persuasion and negotiation)
- If there are conflicts of interest between stakeholders, structure them with the problem-solving frameworks in the **problem** skill
- For managing execution of the whole project, use the frameworks in the **execution** skill
- To understand the motives of those who are against, the perspective of **Motivation-Hygiene Theory** (07) helps

## 07. Motivation-Hygiene Theory (Herzberg)
**Purpose**: Separate motivation factors into "motivators" and "hygiene factors" and design countermeasures

**Template**:
| Factor type | Element | Current state (satisfied/dissatisfied) | Improvement |
|----------|------|-------------|-------|
| **Motivators** | Achievement | | |
| (satisfaction rises when met) | Recognition | | |
| | The work itself | | |
| | Responsibility | | |
| | Growth and promotion | | |
| **Hygiene factors** | Salary | | |
| (dissatisfaction rises when lacking) | Relationships | | |
| | Working conditions | | |
| | Company policy | | |
| | Management methods | | |

**How to use**: Resolve dissatisfaction with hygiene factors first, then strengthen the motivators. Meeting hygiene factors does not raise motivation (it only reduces dissatisfaction). The motivators are the drivers that raise motivation.

### Worked example (analysis of a web engineering team with high turnover)

| Factor type | Element | Current state (satisfied/dissatisfied) | Improvement |
|----------|------|-------------|-------|
| **Motivators** | Achievement | Dissatisfied (releases keep slipping, few success experiences) | Set small sprint goals so the team can feel achievement every 2 weeks |
| (satisfaction rises when met) | Recognition | Dissatisfied (no particular feedback even for good work) | Make contributions visible in a weekly team review. Run a praise channel on Slack |
| | The work itself | Satisfied (technically challenging product) | Keep as is. Encourage writing for the tech blog to create more output opportunities |
| | Responsibility | Dissatisfied (a lot of micromanagement) | Transfer ownership to the team. Write down decision-making authority |
| | Growth and promotion | Dissatisfied (career path unclear) | Define an engineering ladder and hold career interviews every quarter |
| **Hygiene factors** | Salary | Dissatisfied (15% below market rate) | Align with market rate in next term's compensation review. Introduce a performance-linked bonus |
| (dissatisfaction rises when lacking) | Relationships | Satisfied (good atmosphere within the team) | Keep as is. Secure a team-building budget |
| | Working conditions | Dissatisfied (remote work limited to 1 day a week) | Allow remote work 3 days a week. Shorten core hours and expand flextime |
| | Company policy | Satisfied (members identify with the mission) | Continue MVV adoption activities |
| | Management methods | Dissatisfied (evaluation criteria vague) | Make evaluation criteria transparent. Introduce a process of self-evaluation, then manager evaluation, then calibration |

**Priority**: First resolve dissatisfaction with hygiene factors (salary, working conditions, management methods), then strengthen the motivators (achievement, recognition, responsibility, growth)

### Common mistakes
1. **Believing a raise will raise motivation**: Salary is a hygiene factor, so raising it only "reduces dissatisfaction". Above a certain level, redirecting resources to the motivators is more effective
2. **Confusing motivators and hygiene factors**: If "we improved benefits but turnover has not stopped", it is very likely that only hygiene factors were improved and the motivators were neglected
3. **Applying the same countermeasure to everyone**: The priority of motivators differs by person. The effective approach differs between someone who values achievement and someone who values recognition. Interview each person individually in 1on1s

### Tips
- Break engagement survey results down into the 10 items and map them onto this template to make countermeasure design easier
- Keep in mind that "dissatisfaction gets voiced easily, satisfaction does not". If you only pick up complaints and improve hygiene factors, the motivators get overlooked
- From a hiring competitiveness perspective, treat hygiene factors as table stakes (minimum required conditions) and the motivators as differentiators
- Prioritize improvements by "cost" and "impact". Recognition (praise and thanks) costs nothing and works fast

### Next steps
- To dig deeper into an individual member's motives, hold a coaching conversation with the **GROW Model** (09)
- Visualize the balance of members' motivation and ability with the **Will/Skill Matrix** (08)
- If organization-wide policy or culture is the issue, consider redefining the **MVV** (01)
- To plan concrete improvement measures, use the frameworks in the **improvement** skill

## 08. Will/Skill Matrix
**Purpose**: Select a management style according to each member's motivation and ability

**Template**:
|  | Skill: high | Skill: low |
|---|---------|---------|
| **Will: high** | Delegate | Guide |
| **Will: low** | Excite | Direct |

**Details of each quadrant**:

| Quadrant | Characteristics | Management approach | Communication frequency |
|------|------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Delegate** | High motivation, high ability. Can produce results autonomously | Give goals and authority and leave it to them. No need for detailed progress tracking. Evaluate on results | Weekly 1on1. Available for consultation when needed |
| **Guide** | High motivation, low ability. Motivated but lacking skills | Teach carefully. Tolerate failure while raising the difficulty in stages | Daily to 2-3 times a week check-ins. Pair work or a mentor system |
| **Excite** | Low motivation, high ability. Capable but motivation has dropped | Draw out their interests. Give discretion and challenging themes. Dig into "why motivation is low" | Weekly 1on1 with careful listening. Discuss career direction |
| **Direct** | Low motivation, low ability. Needs the most intensive support | Clear instructions and short check cycles. Build up small successes | Daily check-ins. Break tasks into small pieces to create a sense of achievement |

**Member placement**:
| Member | Will | Skill | Quadrant | Management approach |
|---------|------|-------|------|---------------|
| | high/low | high/low | | |

### Worked example (diagnosis of a 5-person marketing team)

| Member | Will | Skill | Quadrant | Management approach |
|---------|------|-------|------|---------------|
| Tanaka (senior) | High | High | Delegate | Fully entrust new channel development. Receive monthly results reports. Expand authority as a candidate for the next leader |
| Sato (mid-level) | Low | High | Excite | Has fallen into a rut recently. Propose leading a new project. Discuss career direction in 1on1s |
| Suzuki (junior) | High | Low | Guide | Plenty of motivation but lacks social media operation skills. On-the-job training under Tanaka as mentor. Study sessions twice a month |
| Takahashi (junior) | High | Low | Guide | Interested in data analysis but lacks experience. Enroll in external training while progressing in stages starting from report creation |
| Watanabe (mid-career hire) | Low | Low | Direct | Struggling with the gap from the previous job. Start by preparing work manuals. Daily 15-minute check-ins |

### Common mistakes
1. **Applying the same management style to all members**: Many leaders fix themselves as "this is my style". A delegating leader neglects members in the Direct quadrant, and a directing leader micromanages members in the Delegate quadrant
2. **Judging Will and Skill by subjective impression alone**: Judge not only by the manager's impression but through dialogue with the person (Will) and performance data (Skill). Will in particular often differs between surface attitude and inner state
3. **Treating quadrants as fixed**: People grow and change. Deliberately design the process by which a member in the "Guide" quadrant builds skills and moves to "Delegate". Also detect early the cases where a member in "Delegate" moves to "Excite" (burnout)

### Tips
- Update the matrix every quarter and track member growth (movement between quadrants)
- For members in the "Excite" quadrant, the top priority is understanding "why Will is low". Causes vary: the work itself, relationships, private life, career anxiety and more
- Members in the "Guide" quadrant give the highest return on investment. With proper support they move to the "Delegate" quadrant the fastest
- If few members on the team are in the "Delegate" quadrant, work tends to concentrate on the leader. A medium-term plan to develop "Delegate" members is essential

### Next steps
- To dig deeper into the motives of members in the "Excite" quadrant, use **Motivation-Hygiene Theory** (07)
- For 1on1 conversations with each member, use the **GROW Model** (09) framework
- To grasp the gap between a member's self-perception and others' perception, the **Johari Window** (04) is effective
- For members' career development, combine with **Will/Can/Must** (02)
- For concrete skill-building plans, use the action plan development in the **execution** skill

## 09. GROW Model
**Purpose**: The basic frame for coaching. Draw out members' self-initiated action through dialogue

**Template**:
| Step | Question | Member's answer |
|---------|------|-------------|
| **G**oal | What do you want to achieve? | |
| **R**eality | What is the situation now? | |
| **O**ptions | What approaches are there? | |
| **W**ill | What specifically will you do? By when? | |

**How to use**: The coach does not give answers but makes the member think through questions. Dig deeper with "What else?" and "Specifically?".

**Detailed example questions for each step**:

| Step | Purpose | Effective example questions |
|---------|------|-----------------|
| **Goal** | Clarify the desired state | - "If you picture the ideal state concretely, what does it look like?" - "When that is achieved, what will have changed?" - "Why is that goal important to you?" - "What would success look like 3 months from now?" |
| **Reality** | Grasp the current situation objectively | - "On a scale of 1 to 10, where are you now?" - "What have you tried so far? What were the results?" - "What is the biggest obstacle?" - "How do you think the people around you see this situation?" |
| **Options** | Expand the possibilities | - "What other approaches can you think of?" (draw out 3 or more) - "If there were no constraints, what would you do?" - "What do you think people who do this well are doing?" - "What are the pros and cons of each option?" |
| **Will** | Commit to action | - "Of those, which do you think is the most effective?" - "Specifically, what is the first step?" - "By when will you do it?" - "What might get in the way of execution? How will you handle it?" - "Is there anything you would like me to support you with?" |

### Worked example (career consultation with a junior engineer)

| Step | Question | Member's answer |
|---------|------|-------------|
| **Goal** | Where do you want to be in six months? | I want to reach a level where I can be entrusted with one feature full-stack, including the front end and not only the back end |
| **Reality** | What is your current skill level? On a scale of 1 to 10? | Back end is 7. Front end is 3. I studied React basics on my own but have no practical experience. There is no dedicated front-end engineer in the company, so I have nobody to consult |
| **Options** | What approaches can you think of? | (1) Take on small front-end tasks in the company (2) Take a practical external online course (3) Build a portfolio with a personal project (4) Look for mentoring from a front-end engineer outside the company |
| **Will** | What will you do first? | Within next week, propose one front-end improvement task for the current product to the PM. In parallel, complete the Udemy React practical course within this month. Report progress in the biweekly 1on1 |

### Common mistakes
1. **Moving on while the Goal is still vague**: With a fuzzy Goal such as "I want to grow" or "I want to do better", everything from Reality onward loses focus. Stay in the Goal step until "by when", "able to do what" and "measured how" are concrete
2. **Not expanding the Options enough**: Settling on "let's go with this" after 1-2 options risks missing the best approach. Draw out at least 3, ideally 5 or more options. Repeat "What else?" persistently
3. **The coach gives advice**: Giving the answer with "why not do it this way?" prevents the member's autonomy from developing. If you really want to give advice, get permission first with "May I make one suggestion?"

### Tips
- A practical approach is to run the GROW Model in the last 15 minutes of a 1on1 meeting. At first, pick one theme as "today's GROW" and talk it through
- It is easier to proceed if you separate the "session goal" from the "final goal". Example: final goal "become a full-stack engineer", session goal "decide one thing to work on this month"
- When the member says "I don't know", do not fear silence. Just waiting 10 seconds often gets them thinking for themselves
- The GROW order does not need to be followed strictly. You may go back from Reality to Goal or from Options to Reality. Prioritize the natural flow of the conversation
- Asking "What was your biggest insight from today's conversation?" at the end of the session helps the learning stick

### Next steps
- To execute the action plan that came out of the GROW Model, use the task management frameworks in the **execution** skill
- To organize the career direction behind the Goal, use **Will/Can/Must** (02)
- To adapt how you use GROW to the member's Will and Skill state, refer to the **Will/Skill Matrix** (08)
- If organization-wide issues come into view during GROW, go back to **Motivation-Hygiene Theory** (07) or the **MVV** (01)
- If the problem needs structuring, break it down logically with the frameworks in the **problem** skill

## How to run the dialogue

### Phase 1: Understand the situation

Confirm the following with AskUserQuestion:

1. **Basic information about the organization**
   - Size of the team/organization (roughly how many people)
   - Industry and business type
   - The user's role (executive, manager, member, etc.)

2. **Identify the issue**
   - "What is the biggest problem in your organization or team right now?"
   - "When did it start?"
   - "Have you already tried anything? What was the result?"

3. **Clarify the goal**
   - "What state do you want to reach through this dialogue?"
   - "What do you want as a concrete output? (analysis results, action plan, checklist, etc.)"

### Phase 2: Select and propose frameworks

Based on the user's issue, propose the 1 to 3 most suitable frameworks. Selection criteria:

| Type of issue | Recommended framework |
|-----------|-----------------|
| The organization's direction is unclear | MVV (01) |
| Members' careers or roles are unclear | Will/Can/Must (02) |
| Cannot prioritize initiatives or resources | Need/Want Matrix (03) |
| Communication breakdown within the team | Johari Window (04) |
| Leadership issues | PM Theory (05) |
| Managing project stakeholders | Stakeholder Analysis (06) |
| Falling member motivation or turnover | Motivation-Hygiene Theory (07) |
| Management methods for each member | Will/Skill Matrix (08) |
| Improving the quality of 1on1s or coaching | GROW Model (09) |

If multiple issues are intertwined, propose a combination of frameworks. Examples:
- "High turnover": analyze causes with Motivation-Hygiene Theory (07), then individual responses with the Will/Skill Matrix (08), then improve 1on1 quality with the GROW Model (09)
- "Launching a new team": set direction with the MVV (01), then organize the people involved with Stakeholder Analysis (06), then check leadership style with PM Theory (05)

### Phase 3: Fill in the template interactively

- Fill in each item of the template together with the user by asking questions
- When the user's answer is vague, dig deeper with "Specifically?" or "For example?"
- Provide related background knowledge or examples from other companies as needed
- If it becomes clear partway through that a different framework is needed, switch flexibly

### Phase 4: Present analysis and insights

Once the template is filled in, provide the following:
- **Summary of the analysis**: patterns and key points that can be read from the template
- **Concrete insights**: perspectives the user may have overlooked
- **Risks and cautions**: problems that could arise if the current situation is left as is

### Phase 5: Action plan and next steps

- **Short-term actions** (within 1-2 weeks): 2-3 concrete actions that can start right away
- **Medium-term actions** (1-3 months): measures involving the setup of systems and structures
- **Long-term actions** (3 months or more): initiatives involving changes in culture and mindset
- **Next framework to use**: propose other frameworks within this skill, or a connection to other skills (strategy, problem, execution, communication, improvement, etc.)

### Phase 6: Output the completed worksheet in Markdown

As the final deliverable, output a Markdown document containing:
- The template of the framework used (filled in)
- Analysis results and insights
- Action plan (with owners and deadlines)
- Proposed next steps
